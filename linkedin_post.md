# LinkedIn Post — Irish Gig Economy Project

---

🇮🇪 Ireland counts 2.8 million people "in employment."

But I wanted to know: how many Irish under-27s doing Deliveroo runs, Fiverr gigs, and zero-hour shifts are invisible to official statistics?

So I built Ireland's first county-level analysis of the "Hidden Work Gap" — the proportion of Gen Z workers whose precarious platform work doesn't appear in standard ILO employment figures.

Here's what the data says 👇

📊 **Key findings:**
• The national hidden work gap grew from 4.1% → 9.5% between 2018–2025
• That's more than doubling in 7 years
• Counties with higher deprivation scores show significantly higher gig participation (r = 0.33)
• Under-25s file the highest proportion of misclassification WRC claims
• Workers win only ~38% of misclassification cases

🗺️ **The most vulnerable counties post-EU Directive:**
Monaghan · Mayo · Wexford · Offaly · Tipperary

⚖️ **Why this matters right now:**
The EU Platform Workers Directive (EU 2024/2831) must be transposed into Irish law by December 2026. It introduces a presumption of employment for platform workers — the biggest change to Irish employment law in a generation.

My model predicts which counties face the highest exposure to this shift.

🛠️ **What I built:**
→ ETL pipeline across 5 public Irish datasets (CSO · Revenue · WRC · Pobal · Google Trends)
→ SQL database with 5 analytical queries
→ 7 publication-quality exploratory charts
→ Logistic Regression risk classifier (F1 = 0.72)
→ Live Streamlit app — "Are you a hidden worker?"

This is what happens when you ask a question official stats don't answer.
